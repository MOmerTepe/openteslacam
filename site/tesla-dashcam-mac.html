HexCam

Tesla dashcam footage on a Mac

How to view dashcam and Sentry Mode clips from the car's USB drive.

A Tesla records its cameras to the USB drive in the glovebox. Plug that drive into a Mac and the clips open in QuickTime, but one camera and one minute at a time. This page says what is on the drive and how to watch it as one recording.

What is on the drive

The drive has a folder named TeslaCam with three folders inside:

  • SavedClips. Dashcam footage you saved, by pressing the dashcam icon or sounding the horn.
  • SentryClips. What Sentry Mode recorded while the car was parked.
  • RecentClips. The last hour or so of driving, overwritten as the car goes on.

Every minute is a separate MP4 file for each camera, named with the time and the camera: 2026-11-20_14-33-00-front.mp4. Newer cars record six cameras (front, back, the two side repeaters and the two door pillars); older ones record four. A ten-minute event on a newer car is sixty files.

Why QuickTime is not enough

  • It plays one file, so you see one camera and lose the others.
  • Each file is a minute long, so anything that crosses a minute is split in two.
  • The speed, steering, braking and position the car writes into the video are not shown at all.

Watching it in HexCam

  1. Plug the car's USB drive into the Mac.
  2. Open the drive, or its TeslaCam folder, in HexCam. Saved, Sentry and recent clips appear as a list of events.
  3. Pick an event. All its cameras play together on one timeline, and the minutes run on as one recording.

From there you can:

  • Arrange the cameras. A grid, one camera large with the rest around it, or a layout of your own.
  • See the car's data. Speed, steering, brake, blinkers and Autopilot state along the timeline, and the route on a map, on clips that carry this data.
  • Import to the Mac. Copy the drive into a library folder, checked file by file, so the drive can go back in the car.
  • Trim and export. Mark a stretch and save it as the original video, or as one video with every camera in it.

Playing, layouts, importing and exporting the original video are free. The car's data is a one-time purchase in the app.

What you need

A Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later. HexCam is a native Mac app: the Mac's own video hardware decodes the six pictures, and the footage never leaves the Mac. See the privacy page.
